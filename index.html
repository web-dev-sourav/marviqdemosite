We believe individuality should never be limited by what everyone else wears or carries. That belief brought our different visions into one, giving life to MarviQ—a brand built to redefine men's essentials through personal choice, individual style, and purpose.

07 / Creative direction

Modern heritage.
Contemporary masculinity.

MarviQ should have the soul of traditional craftsmanship and the confidence of individual expression.

The visual identity combines understated sophistication, strong typography, natural material references, balanced light and dark tones, and clean layouts with intentional negative space. It should feel distinctive and ownable—not like a copy of an existing luxury or leather goods brand.

08 / Strategic color system

The MarviQ palette.

The strategic direction

Modern Heritage with Contemporary Masculinity

MarviQ should feel grounded in the lasting appeal of traditional craftsmanship, while expressing the confidence, freedom, and individuality of the modern man.

We do not recommend building MarviQ around the typical black-and-brown luxury leather aesthetic alone. Although that combination can look attractive, it is widely used across men's leather brands and can make a new identity feel familiar before it has a chance to become distinctive.

MarviQ has a more personal philosophy. It is about individuality, personal choice, freedom, and finding what genuinely represents you. The visual identity should communicate premium craftsmanship without making the brand feel exclusive, intimidating, old-fashioned, or limited to one narrow idea of masculinity.

For that reason, the palette should balance a recognizable signature color with natural warmth, refined neutrals, and purposeful accents. It should feel confident but welcoming, premium but not pretentious, and connected to leather without being defined by leather alone.

Five characteristics that guide the palette

01 / Confidence

Colors should feel strong, grounded, and recognizable. Deep, composed tones establish presence and help MarviQ communicate assurance without relying on aggressive or overly rugged visual cues.

02 / Individuality

The identity needs a distinctive signature color that helps MarviQ stand apart from the familiar black-and-brown category palette. This color should be ownable, memorable, and flexible enough to grow with the brand.

03 / Craftsmanship

Natural warmth should connect the visual identity to leather, material quality, and considered making. Earthy leather-inspired tones bring tactility and authenticity to campaigns, product storytelling, and packaging.

04 / Refinement

The palette should communicate understated sophistication through restraint, balance, and thoughtful contrast—not excessive metallics, decorative effects, or signals of inaccessible luxury.

05 / Timelessness

Colors should remain relevant beyond short-lived trends and support the brand as it expands into new categories. The system must work for leather essentials today and for bags, belts, travel pieces, and broader men's fashion in the future.

Strategic principle: MarviQ's colors should not simply make products look premium. They should express the brand's point of view—individual, confident, refined, and made for what matters.

The resulting palette is a Modern Heritage system: grounded, distinctive, warm, and quietly confident. Deep Forest provides a recognizable signature; Natural Ivory creates openness; Cognac connects the identity to leather and craft.

40% Natural Ivory — main backgrounds
25% Deep Forest — signature presence
15% Carbon Black — typography and contrast
10% Saddle Cognac — leather accents

Why this combination?

Black and brown alone can make a leather brand feel familiar but generic. Deep Forest introduces a more ownable signature while retaining a natural, heritage-inspired character. Ivory keeps the brand approachable and editorial; Cognac makes the connection to leather unmistakable. Together, the palette feels masculine without aggression and premium without pretension.

How not to use the palette

  • Do not use all six colors with equal visual weight. Deep Forest, Ivory and Carbon should do most of the work.
  • Do not use Saddle Cognac, Weathered Stone or Antique Brass for long paragraphs or small text on Ivory.
  • Do not make every section Deep Forest. Alternate light and dark sections to preserve hierarchy and visual rhythm.
  • Do not use Antique Brass as a primary button fill or primary text color.
  • Do not rely on color alone to communicate status, selection or errors; pair color with labels, icons or other visual cues.

Contrast and accessibility note

For normal website text, target a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1; large text can use 3:1 under WCAG 2.1 Level AA. Deep Forest and Carbon Black are the preferred text colors on Natural Ivory. Ivory is the preferred text color on Deep Forest and Carbon Black. Saddle Cognac, Weathered Stone and Antique Brass are accent colors on light backgrounds—not safe defaults for small text.

Contrast ratios calculated from the supplied HEX values: Deep Forest on Ivory ≈ 9.43:1; Carbon Black on Ivory ≈ 13.71:1; Saddle Cognac on Ivory ≈ 3.59:1; Weathered Stone on Ivory ≈ 2.32:1; Antique Brass on Ivory ≈ 3.20:1. Re-check final combinations and states in the production design.

10 / Website typography system

Editorial character. Considered clarity.

This typography system is for MarviQ's website interface and content—not the logo. It pairs Cormorant Garamond as the expressive primary display face with ITC New Baskerville as the secondary reading and interface face. Together they create a more unified, heritage-led editorial voice while preserving a clear distinction between brand expression and practical information.

Primary typeface / Display and editorial
Made for
what matters.

Cormorant Garamond

Role: Primary display typeface. It is the most recognisable typographic expression of MarviQ's brand personality.

Use for: Homepage hero headline, major section headings (H1/H2), campaign headlines, collection titles, editorial statements, short brand-story moments, selected product titles and short pull quotes.

Recommended weights: 500 Medium for most display headings; 600 SemiBold for shorter or compact headings; 400 Regular for softer editorial moments; 400 Italic or 500 Italic for selective emphasis. Use only the styles actually loaded.

Why it stays primary: Its high-contrast, Garamond-influenced forms bring elegance, heritage and a fashion-editorial quality. The delicate hairlines and graceful proportions soften the strength of leather goods and balance MarviQ's masculine, contemporary product direction.

Personality: Expressive, refined, crafted, timeless and distinctive. It should create the emotional first impression rather than carry every practical detail.

Avoid long paragraphs, small navigation labels, prices, dense product specifications and fine print. At small sizes, its fine strokes can lose clarity, especially on lower-quality screens.

Primary / Display serif
Secondary typeface / Reading and interface
Thoughtfully designed essentials for the everyday.

ITC New Baskerville

Role: Secondary typeface and default reading/interface family. It supports the primary display face and carries the information customers need to understand and purchase products.

Use for: Body paragraphs, brand-story supporting copy, product descriptions, material and craftsmanship explanations, product features, size and care information, navigation, buttons, prices, filters, forms, footer links, policies and other utility text.

Recommended weights: 400 Regular for standard reading text. Use 400 Italic selectively for quotations or short editorial emphasis. Use 500 Medium or 600 SemiBold only if those exact styles are included in the licensed font files; do not rely on browser-synthesised bold. If the licensed family has only Regular and Italic, create hierarchy through size, spacing, colour and layout instead.

Why this secondary face: ITC New Baskerville is a classic transitional serif with a composed, structured and literary character. It reinforces MarviQ's crafted, enduring positioning and gives supporting copy a more considered editorial feel than a neutral geometric sans serif. Its role is to communicate with calm authority, not compete with Cormorant's larger display moments.

Personality: Composed, articulate, trustworthy, established and quietly sophisticated. It makes product information feel considered and connected to the brand world.

Because this is a serif-on-serif pairing, test real product pages carefully. Avoid very small, very light or tightly tracked Baskerville text. If a specific interface element becomes difficult to scan, first improve size, contrast and spacing; do not add a third typeface without a deliberate system revision.

Secondary / Text serif

The typographic philosophy

Modern Heritage Through Editorial Typography. MarviQ should feel like a contemporary men's leather-goods label with a lasting point of view—not a generic ecommerce template. Keeping Cormorant Garamond as the primary face preserves the expressive, fashion-led identity already established. Replacing DM Sans with ITC New Baskerville shifts the supporting voice toward a more literary, heritage-informed character and creates continuity between headlines, storytelling and product descriptions.

Two different jobs, one visual language: Cormorant Garamond is the voice of expression—it attracts attention, sets mood and creates memorable brand moments. ITC New Baskerville is the voice of explanation—it carries stories, product information and the practical details that support a confident purchase.

How to maintain hierarchy: Since both families are serif typefaces, do not depend on the font-family change alone to distinguish content levels. Build contrast through scale, weight where licensed, line-height, paragraph width, whitespace, alignment and consistent placement. Keep display headings relatively large and concise; keep supporting text quieter and comfortably readable.

Typesetting and consistency rules

  • Use Cormorant Garamond for short, high-impact display moments—not for every text element.
  • Use ITC New Baskerville for supporting copy and the functional shopping experience, including product information and interface labels.
  • Use sentence case for most headings and sentences. Reserve all caps for very short labels only.
  • Keep paragraph width around 55–75 characters per line where practical to support comfortable reading.
  • Use body line-height around 1.6–1.75; use approximately 1.5–1.7 for compact product copy and specifications.
  • Avoid excessive letter spacing on Baskerville body text. Use tracking mainly for short uppercase labels, and keep it subtle.
  • Create hierarchy with size, whitespace, line-height and alignment before adding more font weights.
  • Keep text colour high-contrast: prefer Carbon Black or Deep Forest on Natural Ivory, and Ivory on Deep Forest or Carbon Black.

Pairing rules and practical cautions

Keep the production website to these two type families. Cormorant Garamond carries the expressive brand personality; ITC New Baskerville carries the reading and information layer. The result should feel like one coherent editorial system, not two competing headline fonts.

Do not use ITC New Baskerville as an oversized display face on every section. Do not use Cormorant for dense product copy, technical details or small controls. Avoid setting both families at similar sizes and weights in adjacent elements, as this weakens the hierarchy.

Check especially the product detail page, where customers must scan product name, price, material, dimensions, care instructions and purchase controls quickly. If the secondary face feels too delicate at a given size, increase its size or weight (only where licensed), improve contrast and spacing, and test again.

Web implementation, loading and licensing

Cormorant Garamond: The primary family is available through Google Fonts and is loaded by this prototype. Keep only the weights and italic styles used in the final design to reduce font requests and file weight.

ITC New Baskerville: Do not assume this commercial typeface is available through Google Fonts. Obtain the webfont files (preferably WOFF2) from the foundry or an authorised font provider and confirm that the licence explicitly covers commercial website use, the intended domain(s), traffic/page-view limits if any, and the required number of domains or seats. Do not upload desktop font files as webfonts unless the licence permits it.

Fallback stack: The CSS uses ITC New Baskerville first, followed by locally available Baskerville and Times New Roman, then a generic serif. These fallbacks are practical substitutes, not exact visual matches. Check line wrapping and spacing when the licensed webfont has not loaded.

Performance: Serve WOFF2 where possible, load only necessary styles, use font-display: swap or an intentionally tested equivalent, and consider preloading only the most important above-the-fold font file. Avoid loading many weights that are not used. Test cumulative layout shift, text wrapping, page speed and rendering on real desktop and mobile devices.

Quality assurance: Review the homepage hero, navigation, product listing cards, product detail page, cart/checkout, footer and long-form policy pages. Test at narrow mobile widths and with the webfont disabled to ensure fallback text remains usable. Confirm all text remains legible at normal zoom and under different display conditions.

Implementation note: the HTML prototype defines ITC New Baskerville as the secondary family with Baskerville/Times New Roman fallbacks. The actual licensed ITC New Baskerville webfont must be supplied by the site owner/developer; it is intentionally not fetched from Google Fonts.

12 / Logo design direction

Distinctive by design.

The logo should communicate confidence, individuality, and lasting style. It must remain clear at small sizes and work across digital platforms and physical product applications.

Primary wordmark

A distinctive MARVIQ wordmark with strong letterform character and excellent legibility.

Secondary logo

A simplified lockup for compact or alternate placements.

Monogram / symbol

An optional ownable symbol derived from the brand name or its philosophy—not a generic fashion emblem.

Product mark

A compact version suitable for labels, embossing, debossing, engraving, and hardware.

The logo should work in monochrome and should not depend on gradients, shadows, or decorative effects. Explore original concepts rather than treating these directions as mandatory solutions.

13 / Usage principles

Consistency creates recognition.

Do

Use color with purpose.

Keep Deep Forest as a signature, Ivory as breathing space, and Cognac as a material cue. Maintain clear contrast and a consistent hierarchy.

Avoid

Making every element compete.

Don't use all six colors equally, overuse metallic accents, or make every section dark. Reserve high contrast for what matters most.

Do

Design for real materials.

Test marks on different leather colors, textures, grain directions, packaging stocks, and hardware finishes.

Avoid

Relying on screen color alone.

HEX values are digital references. Print, foil, leather dye, and metal finishes require physical proofing and material-specific adjustments.

Accessibility and contrast

Use Carbon Black or Deep Forest for important text on Natural Ivory. Do not use Antique Brass for small text on Ivory; its contrast is too low for comfortable reading. Check all interface text, buttons, and interactive states for adequate contrast before launch.
